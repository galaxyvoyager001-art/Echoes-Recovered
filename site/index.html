Source and rights

Every recording comes from the Library of Congress National Jukebox. Each item's Rights & Access statement was checked: recordings published before 1923 are in the US public domain under the Music Modernization Act. Discs recorded before 1922 were cleared under that rule. Discs recorded from 1922 to January 1925 rely on the same statement's 100-year term for 1923–1946 publications, which ended for 1925 issues on 31 December 2025. Recording after February 1925 (the electrical era) is not included. For discs recorded outside the US, only the US status was assessed. Credit: Library of Congress, National Jukebox.

How the restoration works

The channels are folded to mono. Clicks are detected with an autoregressive model and bridged from the real samples on either side. Surface noise is reduced with a decision-directed Wiener filter, hiss above the recorded band is filtered off, and a bounded EQ evens out horn resonances. Nothing is synthesised, and the code and every track's parameters are public.

How to listen

Start either player, then press “Switch” to jump to the other version at the same moment. The label turns while a record plays. “What was removed” plays the exact difference signal, so you can hear that the processing took out noise, not music.
